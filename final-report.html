4.2 didn't cause a slump.
It stranded four responders.

Offers across the roster barely moved. But four responders went from about a dozen offers a week to zero or one, and the way we score them gave them no way back. I'm proposing to fix the score, not undo the release.

What happened In plain words

One bad week, then a loop with no exit.

4.2 made routing rely more on proximity (0.45 → 0.60) and less on acceptance history (0.40 → 0.25), and cut the offer timeout from 90s to 60s. Neither change is the bug. The bug is older: the acceptance score only goes up when someone accepts an offer. Nothing restores it over time. Wen left a TODO about this in 2019.

Why not revert 4.2? The proximity change fixed a real complaint Rook had sat on for three quarters: people losing callouts to someone 40 minutes further away. Reverting would just move the problem to a different group of responders.

How I found it · 02 Super Hearing

One complaint, two different problems.

My promptModule 2 · #12
Are those people who report not receiving calls, actually receiving and accepting or declining them, or just receiving and not seeing them apparently
What it turned up

"My phone never goes off" was the biggest ticket theme. Comparing it with the data split it in two.

Starved: the complaint is accurate. Offers down 79–89%, accepts down 96–100%.

Nightwell group: the data shows offers and accepts rising 21–47% in the same weeks they report silence. An accepted offer must have reached the phone, so a delivery failure doesn't explain it. It looks like offers are being logged to the wrong responder. That's a separate investigation and out of scope here.

How I found it · 04 X-Ray Vision

One way down, one way up, nothing else.

My promptModule 4 · #2
How does the calculation of reliability score look like? Does it get reset over time?
# history.py # TODO(wen, 2019): should this ease back toward # NEUTRAL_SCORE on its own after a while? ... # Leaving it as-is for now. def record_declined(responder): """They turned it down, or we ran out of time waiting. Same either way.""" _set(responder, score - 0.12) def record_accepted(responder): _set(responder, score + 0.08)
What it turned up

In the whole routing codebase, only an accept raises the score. There's no decay or reset, and nothing else changes it. Running the formula against the real weekly counts, three of the four hit exactly 0.0 by 31 Aug. Farlight stopped at 0.12 only because it stopped getting offers.

How I built it · 05 Super Speed

How the prototype changed, prompt by prompt
Success measure in the brief

By week 6 after release, and for the two weeks after, each of the four is sent ≥ 50% of the roster-average weekly pings (≥ 5.5 today, against 0–1 now), measured from pings_sent. This is the same line that unlocks handler priority, so success means none of them qualify for it anymore.

Keeping it from recurring · 06 Sidekicks

A review that runs every Monday.

4.2 shipped a routing reweight and a shorter timeout without a written spec, and nothing I've read set a limit on how few offers any single responder could drop to. The review-checklist skill scores every brief on eight checks. Check 7 fails any brief that doesn't name a guardrail metric.

My promptModule 6 · #6
I like the states the ask, risks and guardrails and claims are evidenced. I also like the sharpening of success measure and scope consistency. Take the current wording of the skill and adjust it so that it includes these additions but stay concise. Give me the adjusted wording before saving it in the skill.
How it's wired in

.claude/skills/review-checklist gives a Pass, Partial or Fail on each check, quotes the brief as evidence, and never edits the brief.

weekly-brief-review runs it every Monday around 09:11 on every brief*.md. Known gap: that pattern misses the .txt briefs in 06-sidekicks/briefs/.
